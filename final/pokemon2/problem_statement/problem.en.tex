\problemname{Pokémon Tournament}
Pokémon master Simone has gathered her friends for a tournament. A
Pokémon match is played between exactly two players and never ends in a draw. It
is also common knowledge that the winner of a Pokémon match receives exactly half of
the opponent's money. In the beginning everyone has $100$ kronor each, and a total
of $M$ matches will be played.

Simone has spied on all her friends, and knows exactly how good they are. She
has ranked all players in a long list, and knows that if two people face
each other, the one who is higher on the list always wins. All players are numbered by
their position on the list. Simone, who is of course the best player, therefore
has number $1$.

She has already published a list of which matches will be played, but
the order has not yet been decided. Now she wonders how
much money she can have at most in the end if she gets to choose in which order
the matches are played. Write a program that computes this!

\section*{Input}
The first line consists of two integers: the number of players (including Simone), $N$,
and the number of matches to be played, $M$.

Then follow $M$ lines with the matches on Simone's list. Each match is a line
with two integers $1 \le a < b \le N$, the numbers of the two players who are to face each other.

No two players will face each other more than once.

\section*{Output}
You should output a decimal number -- the number of kronor Simone can have at the end of
the tournament if she orders the matches optimally. The answer must be given with at least $6$ decimal places of precision.

\section*{Explanation of Sample 1}
Simone does not play any match, so she will only have her original 100
kronor at the end.

\section*{Explanation of Sample 3}
One possible order is to play match 3, then match 2, and finally match 1.

After the first match the players will have (150, 100, 50).

After the second match the players will have (150, 125, 25).

After the third match the players will have (212.5, 62.5, 25).
