\problemname{Ice Skates}
Natalie has bought new ice skates, and has decided to try them at her
local skating rink. The skating rink is shaped like a rectangle, and on the rink
a number of obstacles are placed. Natalie is on the west side of the rink,
and wants to get to the other side (the east).

Natalie is quite bad at ice skating. When Natalie enters the ice
through the entrance she cannot turn until she hits an obstacle. When she
hits the first obstacle she can choose to turn left or right,
and then continue straight ahead, and so on. She always turns
90 degrees left or right when she has hit an obstacle -- and she can only turn
when she has hit an obstacle.

Natalie wants to make the trip as easy as possible. What is the minimum number of turns
she needs to make to get off the ice on the right side (east)?
Natalie always enters the ice at the top-left cell, and initially
travels eastward (to the right).

\section*{Input}
The first line contains the integers $R$ and $C$,
separated by a space.

The next $R$ lines consist of $C$ characters, each describing what a cell
on the skating rink looks like. A '\texttt{.}' means that the cell is empty,
'\texttt{\#}' describes a cell with an obstacle.

When Natalie has exited on the right side of the rink she is done with the trip. If she exits
on any other side of the rink (top, bottom, or left) she fails her
goal. Natalie always starts at cell $(0,0)$ and travels to the right.

\section*{Output}
Your program should output a single number on a line -- the minimum number of turns Natalie
needs to make to get off the ice on the right side. It is guaranteed that
a solution exists.

\section*{Explanation of Samples}
In the third sample (Sample Input 3), Natalie first travels eastward to the first obstacle. She
then turns right and travels downward. She then turns right again and travels westward, to finally
turn right two more times before she reaches the east edge of the ice. A total of four turns; the answer is four.
